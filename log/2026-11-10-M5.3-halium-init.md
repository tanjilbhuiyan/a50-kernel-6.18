### Why the guard's protection error affects userdata

Source inspection explains the observed transition (runtime flag not directly
read): `drivers/ufs/core/ufshcd.c` in `a50-m53-test` reports unsupported opcode
(`scsi_build_sense(... ILLEGAL_REQUEST, 0x20, 0)`) for all denied commands,
including supported WRITE(10) commands denied only because of their address.
`drivers/scsi/scsi_lib.c`, ILLEGAL_REQUEST handling, sees this sense on
WRITE(10), clears the device's `use_10_for_rw` and reprepares the request.
`drivers/scsi/sd.c` then selects WRITE(6) for short writes below LBA 0x200000.
This flag belongs to the whole SCSI device (LUN 0), not the EFS partition, so
it also affects userdata. The guard blocks WRITE(6), and FUA cannot be encoded
in it anyway. Simply allowing WRITE(6) would not resolve the FUA failure.

Next variable: make a denied write report a protection error rather than an
unsupported opcode. Keep the partition allow-list unchanged. Add meaningful
host coverage for the protection response and the no-fallback consequence
alongside the existing guard boundary/mutation tests before building test 3.
